A US Social Security number (SSN) is not the same thing as a US business account requirement. For a US company, most of these providers ask for the company's EIN and the owners' passports. The SSN question matters mostly for sole proprietors and for one provider whose pages disagree. Facts below come from each provider's own pages and the IRS, checked on 2026-11-26. Rules change and every application is reviewed, so verify with the provider before you rely on anything here.

This page is not tax, legal or banking advice. Links to providers on this site are not affiliate links today. See the [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/) for how that would be marked.

| [Wise Business](/provider/wise-business/) | No. SSN is for owners who live in the US or hold a US passport | Photo ID for non-US owners, plus the EIN | [US requirements](https://wise.com/us/blog/wise-business-account-requirements-us) |

All four want an EIN for an LLC or corporation. None of them treats "no SSN" as approval. Each still checks where owners live, the company's address and the business type.

## Sole proprietors are the exception

If you do not form a company, the answer changes:

- **Airwallex:** a sole proprietorship, registered or not, needs the owner's SSN or ITIN.
- **Relay:** sole proprietors provide an SSN, and Relay says it cannot support sole proprietors without a valid SSN or a physical US address.
- **Wise Business:** its US requirements page lists an SSN or TIN if you are a sole proprietor.

Mercury requires a company formed and registered in the US or a US territory, so a sole proprietorship does not fit its rules at all ([eligibility](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility-and-requirements-for-opening-a-Mercury-account)).

## Mercury

Mercury's [documents page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770957425172-Gathering-your-documents) asks for ID for every owner with 25% or more and at least one person with operating control. It says international applicants may provide a passport. It does not list an SSN for them.

For the company, Mercury asks for a state-filed formation document and an IRS EIN document: Form CP575, Form 147c, or an SS-4 returned by the IRS. A screenshot of your EIN confirmation from the IRS website can be accepted if you do not have a completed document yet. Its [EIN page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771757604244-Getting-an-EIN) says you can apply with a completed SS-4 and provide your official EIN within 90 days of approval.

Mercury also needs existing or planned US operations, and it does not accept founders who live in countries on its prohibited list. See the [Mercury provider page](/provider/mercury/) for the list and fees.

## Airwallex

Airwallex's [US documents page](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4419267227417-Documents-required-for-companies-in-the-US) answers this directly. If your business is a company, partnership or other entity type, you do not need an SSN or ITIN to apply. As a non-US person, you verify your identity with your passport or another government photo ID and enter the business EIN as its Tax ID.

The harder rules are elsewhere:

- The company needs a genuine US principal place of business. A registered agent address is accepted for this only if the business sells goods online or was incorporated within the last year. PO boxes and virtual mailboxes are never accepted.
- No owner or ultimate beneficial owner may live in a restricted country or territory.
- For foreign-owned businesses, Airwallex may ask for more proof of US activity, such as a recent statement from a brick-and-mortar US bank. It says US neobank statements, such as Mercury statements, are not accepted for that request.

## Wise Business

Wise's [US requirements page](https://wise.com/us/blog/wise-business-account-requirements-us) (last updated 31 July 2026) says an SSN is required for beneficial owners who live in the US or hold a US passport. Non-US beneficial owners provide a copy of a photo ID. The business provides its EIN.

Wise is a money services business, not a bank. For founders abroad the bigger question is often whether you can get USD account details. Wise's [USD details page](https://wise.com/help/articles/2810318/can-i-get-usd-account-details) says you cannot get them if your address is in a listed place. On 2026-11-26 that list included Bangladesh, Nigeria, Pakistan, Serbia, Sri Lanka, Türkiye, the United Arab Emirates and Ukraine, among others. It also says a US business with an address in Bangladesh, Cameroon, China, Nigeria or Pakistan cannot get USD details. Wise says it cannot onboard businesses located in Nevada or in US territories.

With USD details, Wise lists no fee for ACH, and 6.11 USD to receive a domestic wire or a Swift payment ([source](https://wise.com/help/articles/2827506/how-do-i-receive-money-with-my-usd-account-details)).

## Relay

Relay's two help pages, both marked updated November 12, 2026, disagree:

- [Required documents by entity type](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/38049251551636-Required-Documents-and-Organization-Details-to-Open-a-Relay-Account-by-Entity-Type/) asks every beneficial owner for an "SSN or passport number", and passport only as ID for non-US citizens.
- [How to open a Relay account](https://support.relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/38050807932436-How-to-Open-a-Relay-Business-Account) says every beneficial owner must have a valid SSN, and the sign-up flow asks you to confirm you have one.

We mark Relay's SSN rule for non-US owners as unknown. Two other Relay rules matter for founders abroad. The required documents page asks every beneficial owner for a physical US address, with no PO boxes or virtual mailboxes. And Relay's [prohibited countries page](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/10239600121748-Prohibited-Countries/) blocks owners who hold citizenship or residency in listed countries, including Nigeria, Pakistan and Ukraine.

## Getting an EIN without an SSN

Every provider above wants the company's EIN. You can get one without an SSN or ITIN:

- **Online:** the IRS [online EIN tool](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number) only works if your principal place of business is in the US and you have the responsible party's SSN or ITIN. Most founders abroad cannot use it.
- **Line 7b:** the [Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4) say to enter "foreign" or N/A on line 7b if the responsible party does not have and is ineligible to obtain an SSN or ITIN.
- **Phone:** if you have no legal residence, principal place of business or principal office in the US or a US territory, you can call 267-941-1099 (not toll-free), 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. Eastern, Monday to Friday. The IRS says only international applicants can get an EIN by phone.
- **Fax:** generally within 4 business days. Applicants without a US residence or place of business fax to 304-707-9471 from outside the US.
- **Mail:** about 4 weeks, to Internal Revenue Service, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999.

The IRS says it never charges a fee for an EIN, and it issues one EIN per responsible party per day. Form the company with the state before you apply.

## What an ITIN is, and when it matters

The IRS says an [ITIN](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/individual-taxpayer-identification-number) is a 9-digit number for people who need a US taxpayer identification number for federal tax purposes but are not eligible for an SSN. It is for federal tax only and does not serve as ID outside the federal tax system. You generally apply with a tax return, by mail or in person. The IRS says to allow 7 weeks, or 9 to 11 weeks if you apply from overseas or in tax season.

For opening a business account for a US company, none of the provider pages above asks a non-US owner for an ITIN. An ITIN matters for sole proprietors at Airwallex and for using the IRS online EIN tool. Whether you need one for your own US tax filings is a question for an accountant.

## Before you apply

- Form the company first, then get the EIN, then apply. Airwallex and Relay both want the EIN in the application.
- Check where every 25% owner lives against each provider's country list. No SSN requirement does not override a country ban.
- Have a passport for every owner and an address the provider accepts. Registered agent and virtual addresses fail at several of these firms.
- Expect manual review and requests for more documents.

Stripe does not support Pakistan, so most Pakistani founders and freelancers get paid through a merchant of record, a payments account such as Payoneer, or a mix. This page lists what each provider's own pages say about Pakistan, checked on 2026-11-08. Policies change, and applying does not guarantee approval, so verify with the provider before you rely on any option.

This page is not tax, legal or banking advice. It does not cover State Bank of Pakistan or FBR rules for receiving foreign income, so check those with a qualified local accountant. Links to providers on this site are not affiliate links today. See the [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/) for how that would be marked.

## Which route fits what you sell

| You sell or do | Routes to look at first | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Software, SaaS or apps to buyers worldwide | A merchant of record that lists Pakistan: Paddle, Lemon Squeezy or Polar | They charge customers and handle sales tax and VAT, then pay you out. You do not need Stripe in Pakistan |
| eBooks, PDFs, design assets | Lemon Squeezy or Polar, if they accept your product | Both name these as acceptable. Paddle is for software |
| Freelance or agency services paid by clients or marketplaces | Payoneer, or Wise Business where Wise supports your business | Lemon Squeezy, Paddle and Polar all exclude or discourage services |
| A US LLC with a US bank account | Check each bank's residence rules first | Mercury lists Pakistan as prohibited by residence |

### Paddle

Paddle says it works with software businesses anywhere except the countries on its unsupported list. Pakistan is not on that list. Paddle says it is built for software companies (B2B SaaS, consumer software and games). If your main offering is human services such as consultation, support or design, or physical goods, Paddle says it is not a good fit. Its [acceptable use page](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle) was last updated 13 April 2026 and lists many other prohibited and restricted categories.

Paddle pays out once a month, by the 15th, and there are no on-demand withdrawals. You can be paid by wire or Payoneer. For most countries Paddle charges no payout fee, but it says a $15 SWIFT fee may apply in certain countries. Whether that fee applies to Pakistan is unknown, because the page does not name the countries.

### Lemon Squeezy

Lemon Squeezy lists Pakistan among the countries where bank payouts are supported. Its page also says PayPal payouts are supported in 200+ countries and regions. Whether Pakistan is among them is unknown here, because Lemon Squeezy refers to PayPal's own list. If your country is on neither list, Lemon Squeezy says you cannot use it.

Its [prohibited products page](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products) lists services of any kind, including marketing, design, web development and consulting. Acceptable examples include software and SaaS, eBooks, PDFs, design assets, photos, audio and video. See [Lemon Squeezy alternatives](/guides/lemon-squeezy-alternatives/) for how it compares with other merchants of record.

### Polar

Polar pays out through Stripe Connect Express to residents and businesses in the countries on its list, and Pakistan is on it. Polar says this works even where regular Stripe payments are unavailable, because Polar is the merchant of record and only uses Stripe for payouts. It also says Stripe Connect Express must support your business type in your country for individual sellers. We did not check Stripe's Connect requirements for Pakistan, so whether an individual can register is unknown here.

Polar's [acceptable use policy](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/acceptable-use) (effective October 1, 2026 on that page) allows software and SaaS, digital products and premium content. It excludes human services and physical goods. eBooks, AI content generation tools and marketing tools are listed as needing closer review.

### Dodo Payments

Dodo says eligibility depends on the country that issued the government ID you verify with. It does not depend on where your company is registered or where you pay tax. Pakistan is not on the accepted list, and Dodo says payouts are available only in the countries on it. If you hold an ID issued by a listed country, ask Dodo support before you build.

## Payoneer

Payoneer publishes registration guides for Pakistan, last modified August 31 and September 1, 2026. They describe three account types: Individual (no business registration), Sole Proprietor (FBR/NTN registration) and Company (SECP registration). Payoneer says it is not a bank.

For an Individual account Payoneer lists these requirements:

- A government photo ID (National ID card, passport or driver's license) and a selfie.
- A private residential address, shown on a document in your name. P.O. boxes are not accepted.
- A bank account in your exact registered name, used for withdrawals. Payoneer asks for the IBAN, which usually starts with PK.

Payoneer says applications are usually reviewed within 3 business days after all documents are submitted, that only one account per person is allowed, and that accounts can be declined or closed. Payoneer says Individual accounts can receive payments from marketplaces such as Upwork and Fiverr and from direct clients by payment request, and can withdraw to a local Pakistani bank account.

Fees on Payoneer's [pricing page](https://www.payoneer.com/pricing/) (last updated 1 January 2026 on that page):

- Receiving through a local-currency receiving account: free. Through a receiving account in a non-local currency: 1% (minimum $1).
- Receiving from a payer using a card: up to 3.99% + 49 cents.
- Withdrawing to a bank account: 1.2% to 4%, and a minimum of up to $20 may apply in some countries.
- Annual account fee of $29.95 if the account receives less than $6,000 in any 12 consecutive months.

Pakistan is not on Payoneer's list of countries with a flat local-currency withdrawal fee. The exact withdrawal fee for a Pakistani bank is unknown here. Payoneer says the fee for your account is shown inside your account before you confirm a transaction.

## Wise and US banks

Wise's [where can I use Wise](https://wise.com/help/articles/2978049/where-can-i-use-wise) page lists the places it does not serve. Pakistan is not on it. Wise says it tells you whether it supports businesses in your country when you add your details, so business eligibility in Pakistan is unknown until you reach that step.

Wise's [USD account details page](https://wise.com/help/articles/2810318/can-i-get-usd-account-details) lists Pakistan among the countries where you cannot get USD account details if your address is there. It adds that a US business with an address in Pakistan cannot get them either. Wise is a money services business, not a bank. More on [Wise Business](/provider/wise-business/).

Mercury's [prohibited countries page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries) lists Pakistan. Mercury says it cannot open accounts for founders who live in listed countries, based on residence and not citizenship. If you are a citizen of a listed country but live elsewhere, Mercury says your application may still be eligible after review. Forming a US LLC does not change that. See [US bank account for a non-resident LLC](/guides/us-bank-account-for-non-resident-llc/) for the other banks.

## Before you choose

- Match the route to what you sell. Merchants of record take software and digital products. Services usually go through client payments or marketplaces.
- Confirm each provider's current country list on the day you apply. Dodo's page, for example, says its country policy applies to designations made on or after March 23, 2026.
- Read the product rules. Several providers reject or review AI tools, eBooks, directories and marketing tools.
- Check the payout path end to end: provider, payout method, fee, and what your Pakistani bank will accept.
- Ask a local accountant about tax and State Bank of Pakistan rules before you take money from overseas.

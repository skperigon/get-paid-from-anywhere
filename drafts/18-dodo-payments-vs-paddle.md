Dodo Payments and Paddle are both merchants of record: they sell to your customer, handle sales tax and VAT, and pay you out. Both serve software sellers. They differ on fees, payout schedule, which countries they accept and which products they allow. Facts below come from each provider's own pages, checked on 2026-11-01.

## Fees compared

| Fee (checked 2026-11-01) | [Dodo Payments](/provider/dodo-payments/) | [Paddle](/provider/paddle/) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base card rate | 4% + 40 cents for US domestic cards and wallets | 5% + 50 cents per checkout transaction |
| International cards | +1.5% | No surcharge listed. Paddle says its pricing is all-inclusive |
| Subscriptions | +0.5% | No surcharge listed |
| PayPal | +3% | No surcharge listed |
| Buy now, pay later | +3% | unknown. Not listed on Paddle's pricing page |
| Bank debits | ACH and SEPA: 1.5%, capped at $15 or 15 euros | unknown. Not listed on Paddle's pricing page |
| Disputes | $30 | unknown. Paddle lists chargeback handling as included but shows no fee |
| Refunds | $1 per refund | unknown. Not listed on Paddle's pricing page |
| Monthly fee | None | None |
| Low-price products | Not listed | Products under $10 can contact Paddle for custom pricing |

Sources: [Dodo pricing](https://dodopayments.com/pricing) and [Paddle pricing](https://www.paddle.com/pricing). Dodo also lists 4% + 15 cents for domestic INR payments in India, plus international fees. Both offer custom pricing for high volume.

### Worked examples

Our own arithmetic on the published rates, for a $30 sale, before tax and payout costs:

- One-time sale, US card: Dodo 4% of $30 plus 40 cents = $1.60. Paddle 5% of $30 plus 50 cents = $2.00.
- One-time sale, non-US card: Dodo 5.5% of $30 plus 40 cents = $2.05. Paddle $2.00.
- Monthly subscription, non-US card: Dodo 6% of $30 plus 40 cents = $2.20. Paddle $2.00.

So Dodo's lower base rate wins on domestic US sales, and Paddle's flat rate wins once the international and subscription surcharges stack up.

## How you get paid

- **Dodo Payments:** standard payouts are free, with a $5 fee on payouts under $1,000. Standard FX applies to non-USD settlements. USD payouts by SWIFT cost $25.
- **Paddle:** payouts run once a month. Your balance converts to a payout on the 1st if it is over your threshold (minimum $100), and Paddle sends it by the 15th. You cannot withdraw on demand. You can be paid by wire transfer or Payoneer. Paddle adds no payout fee of its own, but a wire in a currency different from your bank's country goes by SWIFT for a $15 fee (or 15 euros or pounds), and Paddle may add a conversion margin of up to 1.5% if you take a payout in a currency other than your balance currency. Payoneer fees are yours.

Sources: [Paddle payout schedule](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/when-and-how-do-i-get-paid) and [Paddle payout fees](https://www.paddle.com/help/manage/get-paid/is-there-a-fee-taken-for-payouts).

## Who can sign up

- **Dodo Payments:** eligibility goes by the country that issued your government ID. For a company, Dodo checks the country of incorporation plus the ID country of every director and beneficial owner. Registering a company in a listed country does not by itself make you eligible, per its [accepted countries page](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/accepted-countries-and-territories). Since March 23, 2026, merchants from Bangladesh, Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Morocco, Nauru, Nigeria, Tuvalu and Ukraine are grandfathered: existing merchants stay, new ones are not accepted.
- **Paddle:** Paddle says it works with software businesses anywhere except a [list of unsupported countries](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/which-countries-are-supported-by-paddle). On 2026-11-01 that list included Afghanistan, Belarus, Burma (Myanmar), Central African Republic, Cuba, Democratic Republic of Congo, Haiti, Iran, Iraq, Libya, Mali, Nicaragua, North Korea, Russia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Yemen and Zimbabwe, plus the Crimea, Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk and Zaporizhzhia regions of Ukraine. Paddle says the list can change and it may ask for more information before releasing a payment.

## What you can sell

- **Dodo Payments:** its [merchant acceptance page](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/merchant-acceptance) welcomes SaaS and AI products, digital goods, and templates, plugins and apps. Prohibited categories include manually delivered digital services, physical goods, gaming and virtual-goods environments, hosting, servers and VPNs, and marketplaces. AI content generation, marketing and outreach tools, e-books and productised services often need extra review.
- **Paddle:** its [acceptable use policy](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle) says Paddle is built for software companies, including B2B SaaS, consumer software and games. It prohibits physical products, human services not tied to software, digital marketplaces, advertising and marketing services, automated social media marketing, gambling, regulated financial services, and some kinds of AI content generation such as face swaps, deepfakes and voice impersonation, among others.

If your product is near any of these lines, read the full policy before you apply.

## Which to pick

- Mostly one-time sales to US buyers: Dodo Payments has the lower rate.
- Subscriptions sold mostly to buyers outside the US: Paddle's flat 5% + 50 cents is likely lower than Dodo's stacked rate.
- Seller in Pakistan, Kenya, Nigeria, Bangladesh, Egypt or Morocco: Paddle, since Dodo does not take new merchants from these countries.
- Seller in Zimbabwe or Mali: Dodo Payments, since Paddle lists these countries as unsupported.
- Selling games: Paddle names games as a fit, while Dodo prohibits gaming and virtual-goods environments.
- Need cash more than once a month: Dodo, since Paddle pays out monthly.

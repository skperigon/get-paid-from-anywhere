Mercury is one of the names that comes up most when founders outside the US ask where to bank a US company. Its help center sets out who can apply. This page collects those rules in one place, all checked on Mercury's own pages on 2026-11-25. Mercury reviews every application and can change its rules, so verify with Mercury before you rely on anything here.

This page is not tax, legal or banking advice. Links to providers on this site are not affiliate links today. See the [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/) for how that would be marked.

## The short answer

Mercury does not require you to be a US citizen or resident. It does require three things ([eligibility page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility-and-requirements-for-opening-a-Mercury-account)):

1. A company formed and registered in the United States or a US territory.
2. Existing or planned operations in the US.
3. Founders who do not live in a country on Mercury's [prohibited list](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries).

Mercury is a fintech company, not a bank. It says banking services are provided through Choice Financial Group and Column N.A., Members FDIC.

## Prohibited countries: the residence rule

Mercury says it cannot open accounts for founders who live in, or businesses that operate or do business in, the countries on its list. The rule is about where you live, not your citizenship. A citizen of a listed country who lives elsewhere may still be eligible after review.

The list has two parts. Sanctioned: Cuba, Iran, North Korea and the Russian-occupied regions of Ukraine. Prohibited: 48 countries, including several that founders often ask about, such as Bangladesh, Indonesia, Nepal, Nigeria, Pakistan, the Philippines and Vietnam. It also includes some European countries, such as Croatia, Latvia and Ukraine. Read the full list on Mercury's page, since it changes.

## Who has to be listed

Mercury collects details for every beneficial owner, meaning anyone who owns 25% or more directly or through another company. If a holding company owns part of your business, you list the people who own the holding company. You also name one person with significant responsibility for running the company, who becomes the account admin. Everyone listed must be 18 or older.

For each beneficial owner, Mercury asks for the physical address where that person lives. PO boxes, mail drops and virtual address services are not accepted, and Mercury may ask for a document to verify it.

## Businesses Mercury does not accept

Mercury's eligibility page says it cannot open accounts for money services businesses and crypto exchanges, adult entertainment, cannabis, internet gambling, or trusts. It supports many crypto and Web3 startups, but accounts hold fiat currency only. Other industries may need more information.

## Documents

Mercury's [documents page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770957425172-Gathering-your-documents) lists:

- **Formation document:** state-filed papers such as Articles of Organization, Articles of Incorporation or a Certificate of Formation.
- **EIN document:** IRS Form CP575, Form 147c, or a returned SS-4. A screenshot of your EIN confirmation from the IRS website can be accepted if you do not have a completed document yet.
- **ID:** for every owner with 25% or more and at least one person with operating control. International applicants may provide a passport.

Mercury says it can sometimes verify these details automatically, so you may not be asked to upload all of them. Its [EIN page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771757604244-Getting-an-EIN) says you can apply with a completed SS-4 and provide your official EIN within 90 days of approval. The documents page does not list an SSN for international applicants.

## Address rules

Mercury's [address page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28769699533588-Company-address-requirements) separates three addresses:

- **Legal address:** the address on your formation documents. It must be a physical address in the United States and cannot be a PO box.
- **Physical address:** where you and your team work. A verifiable residential address or a verifiable international address is accepted. A PO box, virtual address, mail receiving agency, mail center or registered agent address is not. Mercury may ask you to verify this address if your legal address is a registered agent or virtual address.
- **Mailing address:** where Mercury sends physical cards. It must be a physical address that the US Postal Service can deliver to.

For the physical address, Mercury may ask for proof such as a lease, a utility bill or bank statement from the last 90 days (not from Mercury), a coworking invoice, or tax documents.

The pricing page does not list a separate Mercury fee for receiving international wires, so we mark that as unknown. The sender's bank and intermediary banks may charge their own fees.

## Getting paid from abroad

Mercury accounts are USD only. Its [non-USD payments page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/34750478414740-Receiving-non-USD-payments) says you can receive international wires in 139 non-USD currencies, and they are converted to USD before they reach your account. How you are converted depends on the partner bank that holds your account:

- **Column N.A.:** converts at the mid-market rate.
- **Choice Financial Group:** uses third-party intermediary banks that typically add a 2% to 3% markup to the exchange rate.

Mercury says it does not set or profit from these conversions. Your wire details, including separate instructions for USD and non-USD international wires, are in the dashboard ([source](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28767997786260-Finding-your-wire-details)).

## If you are rejected

Mercury says it does not share the specific reasons for a decision. If you have new information, you can [reapply](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/31759083693844-Reapplying-if-your-Mercury-application-has-been-denied) from your denied application's settings. Approval is not guaranteed.

## If Mercury is not an option

If you live in a prohibited country, a different US state or company structure does not change that, because the rule follows where you live. Other providers publish their own lists and rules. Compare Mercury, Wise Business, Airwallex and Relay in [US bank account for a non-resident LLC](/guides/us-bank-account-for-non-resident-llc/).

You can also skip the US company and sell through a merchant of record that pays out to your own country. See [merchant of record for SaaS](/sell/saas/) and your country page.

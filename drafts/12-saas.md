A merchant of record (MoR) sells your SaaS to the customer on your behalf. It collects the payment, handles sales tax and VAT in the markets it covers, and pays you the remainder after its fees. That is different from a payment processor such as standard Stripe, where you stay the merchant and own tax registration and remittance yourself.

This page covers how that model works for subscription software, which providers offer it, and what their own pricing and product pages say about SaaS. Facts were checked on 2026-11-06. Fees and acceptance rules change, so verify with the provider before you rely on any figure.

## Why SaaS founders use a merchant of record

For a subscription product sold in many countries, the recurring work is tax, payment method coverage, refunds, chargebacks and buyer billing support. An MoR takes that stack as the seller of record. You keep product, pricing and customer relationship for the software itself.

Trade-offs that show up on the providers' own pages:

- Higher take rate than a raw card processor in many cases, especially once subscription or international surcharges apply.
- Product and country gates. Each MoR publishes what it will and will not sell, and where sellers can be based.
- You are not the merchant on the customer's receipt. That helps with tax, and it also means the MoR can refund or refuse categories under its own policies.

### Worked examples on a $29 monthly plan

Our own arithmetic on the published rates, before tax and payout costs:

| $29 renewal | Paddle | Lemon Squeezy | Polar Starter | Polar Early Member | Dodo Payments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US card | $1.95 | $2.10 | $1.95 | $1.71 | $1.71 |
| Non-US card | $1.95 | $2.53 | $2.39 | $2.14 | $2.14 |

How those numbers were built:

- Paddle: 5% + 50 cents on both rows.
- Lemon Squeezy US: 5% + 50 cents + 0.5% subscription. Non-US: same plus 1.5% international.
- Polar Starter: 5% + 50 cents, plus 1.5% on the non-US row. No subscription surcharge on Starter.
- Polar Early Member: 4% + 40 cents + 0.5% subscription, plus 1.5% on the non-US row.
- Dodo: 4% + 40 cents + 0.5% subscription, plus 1.5% on the non-US row.

On this price point, Paddle's flat rate is cheapest once the buyer is outside the US. On US cards, Dodo and Polar Early Member undercut Paddle; Lemon Squeezy is highest of the five columns because of the subscription add-on.

Paddle and Lemon Squeezy both say sellers of products under $10 should ask about custom pricing. Polar's paid plans cut the variable rate once monthly volume is high enough that the plan fee pays for itself.

## Product fit for SaaS

### Paddle

Paddle's [acceptable use page](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle) (last updated 13 April 2026 on that page) says it is built to serve software companies, including B2B SaaS, consumer software and games. If the primary offering is human services or physical goods, Paddle says it is not a good fit.

Prohibited categories that often trip SaaS-adjacent products include pure consulting, donations with no software sold, marketplaces that let other sellers take payments, adult content, gambling, many financial products and advice categories, medical advice, and several AI content-generation patterns (face swaps, deepfakes, voice impersonation). Restricted categories need extra review. Confirm the live list.

### Lemon Squeezy

Lemon Squeezy's [prohibited products page](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products) lists Software and SaaS among acceptable examples, along with eBooks, PDFs and design assets. It prohibits services of any kind, physical goods, marketplaces that sell others' products, NFT and crypto products, and a long list of regulated categories. Lemon Squeezy's [MoR docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/merchant-of-record) say it acts as merchant of record and takes on tax, refunds, chargebacks and PCI scope for sales through the platform.

### Polar

Polar's [acceptable use policy](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/acceptable-use) (effective date on that page: October 1, 2026) lists Software and SaaS under acceptable products, plus digital products and premium content or access. It says Polar serves software companies and is not designed for primary human services or physical goods. Prohibited and restricted lists cover marketplaces, adult content, gambling, many financial services, medical advice, and several AI generation categories that need closer review.

### Dodo Payments

Dodo's [merchant acceptance policy](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/merchant-acceptance) says it welcomes SaaS and AI products, digital goods, and templates, plugins and apps. It prohibits manual digital services where most of the value is human labour, physical goods, many financial and professional services, gambling, crypto, hosting and VPN-style infrastructure, and a long set of other categories. Some AI tools and eBooks need extra review rather than automatic approval.

### Stripe Managed Payments

Stripe's [Managed Payments eligibility docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility) support digital products, including SaaS tax codes for personal and business use. The business must be based in a supported location (US, Canada, a listed set of European countries, Australia, Hong Kong, Japan or Singapore). Connect platforms and Express accounts are not supported. Products must be fully automated digital goods; services with human intervention such as live 1-1 coaching do not qualify. Stripe reviews each business for access.

## How to choose for a SaaS subscription

| Situation | Start with (still verify) |
| --- | --- |
| Software-only SaaS, many non-US buyers, want one flat published rate | Paddle |
| SaaS plus downloads or digital goods in one store | Lemon Squeezy or Polar |
| US-card-heavy volume and you want the lowest published US card rate among these four | Dodo Payments (watch the +0.5% on subscriptions) |
| Already on Polar before May 27, 2026 and still on Early Member | Polar Early Member rate, until you leave it |
| Business based in a Managed Payments location and you already use Stripe | Stripe Managed Payments, after eligibility review |
| Consulting, agencies or human-delivered services as the main product | None of these MoRs are a fit per their own policies |

Country eligibility is a separate gate from product fit. Seller-country detail for these providers is covered in [Paddle alternatives](/guides/paddle-alternatives/), [Lemon Squeezy supported countries](/guides/lemon-squeezy-supported-countries/) and [Dodo Payments supported countries](/guides/dodo-payments-supported-countries/).

## What this page does not cover

- Your home-country income tax on MoR payouts. Ask a local tax professional.
- Exact payout timing and payout fees beyond what each fee page states. See the comparison guides linked above for payout tables checked on earlier dates.
- Approval odds. Meeting the published product and country rules does not guarantee an account.

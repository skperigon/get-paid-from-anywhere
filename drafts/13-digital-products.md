A merchant of record (MoR) sells your digital product to the customer on your behalf. It collects the payment, handles sales tax and VAT in the markets it covers, and pays you the remainder after its fees. That is different from a payment processor such as standard Stripe, where you stay the merchant and own tax registration and remittance yourself.

This page covers ebooks, PDFs, templates, design assets, pre-recorded courses and similar downloads. For subscription software, see [Merchant of record for SaaS](/sell/saas/). Facts were checked on 2026-11-07. Fees and acceptance rules change, so verify with the provider before you rely on any figure.

## Why digital-product sellers use a merchant of record

A file sold in many countries still creates tax, payment-method, refund, chargeback and buyer-billing work. An MoR takes that stack as the seller of record. You keep the product, the pricing and the customer relationship for the file itself.

Trade-offs that show up on the providers' own pages:

- Higher take rate than a raw card processor in many cases, especially once international or (if you sell memberships) subscription surcharges apply.
- Product and country gates. Each MoR publishes what it will and will not sell, and where sellers can be based. Those lists are stricter for human services and physical goods than they are for finished files.
- You are not the merchant on the customer's receipt. That helps with tax, and it also means the MoR can refund or refuse categories under its own policies.

## Fees for one-time downloads

Most ebooks, templates and pre-recorded courses are one-time checkout, not a monthly renewal. That matters because some MoRs add a subscription surcharge only when the payment is a subscription. Here is what each provider's fee page said on 2026-11-07.

| Fee | Paddle | Lemon Squeezy | Polar | Dodo Payments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base rate | 5% + 50 cents | 5% + 50 cents | Starter 5% + 50 cents; Pro 3.8% + 40 cents ($20/mo); Growth 3.6% + 35 cents ($100/mo); Scale 3.4% + 30 cents ($400/mo) | 4% + 40 cents (US domestic cards and wallets) |

| Monthly platform fee | None | None for ecommerce | None on Starter; paid plans as above | None |

### Worked examples on a $29 one-time download

Our own arithmetic on the published rates, before tax and payout costs. This is the same price point used on the [SaaS page](/sell/saas/), shown here as a one-time file sale so the subscription add-ons drop off.

| $29 one-time download | Paddle | Lemon Squeezy | Polar Starter | Polar Early Member | Dodo Payments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| US card | $1.95 | $1.95 | $1.95 | $1.56 | $1.56 |
| Non-US card | $1.95 | $2.39 | $2.39 | $2.00 | $2.00 |

How those numbers were built:

- Paddle: 5% + 50 cents on both rows.
- Lemon Squeezy US: 5% + 50 cents. Non-US: same plus 1.5% international. No subscription add-on on a one-time sale.
- Polar Starter: 5% + 50 cents, plus 1.5% on the non-US row. No subscription surcharge on Starter.
- Polar Early Member: 4% + 40 cents, plus 1.5% on the non-US row.
- Dodo: 4% + 40 cents, plus 1.5% on the non-US row.

If you instead sell that $29 product as a subscription (for example a course membership), Lemon Squeezy and Dodo each add 0.5%. Polar adds 0.5% only on Early Member. On a US card that would bring Lemon Squeezy to $2.10 and Dodo or Polar Early Member to $1.71, matching the figures on the [SaaS fee table](/sell/saas/).

Paddle and Lemon Squeezy both say sellers of products under $10 should ask about custom pricing. Polar's paid plans cut the variable rate once monthly volume is high enough that the plan fee pays for itself.

## Product fit for ebooks, templates and courses

This is the main difference from [MoR for SaaS](/sell/saas/). The same providers treat finished files, pre-recorded courses and human-delivered services differently.

### Paddle

Paddle's [acceptable use page](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/what-am-i-not-allowed-to-sell-on-paddle) (last updated 13 April 2026 on that page) says it is built to serve software companies, including B2B SaaS, consumer software and games. If the primary offering is human services or physical goods, Paddle says it is not a good fit. Physical products and human services that are not related to a software offering are prohibited.

That page does not list ebooks, templates or courses as acceptable examples. Other official Paddle pages do discuss those product types:

- The [digital products page](https://www.paddle.com/solutions/sell-digital-products) says you can scale e-books, courses and downloadable files, and lists e-books, courses, audio and music files under digital products.
- The [developer docs for digital products](https://developer.paddle.com/get-started/how-paddle-works/digital-products) describe selling e-books, software licenses, courses, plugins and other downloadable files.
- A [taxable categories help article](https://www.paddle.com/help/start/intro-to-paddle/why-do-i-need-to-select-'taxable-categories'-for-my-products) includes an eBooks category (digital books and educational material sold with permanent rights) and a Digital Goods category. Digital Goods examples include online video tutorials and digital artwork, posters or graphics.

Templates are not named on those pages. The acceptable use page also prohibits certifications or courses for which you are not the owner or creator, or do not have authority to resell.

Paddle can be a fit for a software-adjacent download or a course it is willing to treat as a digital product. It is not a clear, list-style yes for a templates-only store the way Lemon Squeezy, Polar and Dodo are. Ask Paddle if your catalog is mostly ebooks or templates and not software.

### Lemon Squeezy

Lemon Squeezy's [prohibited products page](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/prohibited-products) says it allows digital goods that can be fulfilled through its website. Acceptable examples listed there are Software and SaaS, eBooks, PDFs, design assets, photos, audio and video. Courses are not named on that acceptable list.

Two other official pages do mention courses. The [pricing FAQ](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/pricing) says you can sell digital products ranging from SaaS, digital downloads, subscriptions, memberships, courses and more. The [activate-your-store docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/activate-your-store) say Lemon Squeezy approves most stores selling digital goods it can fulfill (eBooks, PDFs, design assets, photos, audio, video and similar), and that businesses using subscriptions or license keys to manage fulfilment (examples include premium courses and membership sites) are typically allowed.

Lemon Squeezy prohibits services of any kind, including marketing, design, web development and consulting. Physical goods, marketplaces that sell others' products, NFT and crypto products, and a long list of regulated categories are also prohibited. A live coaching call or custom design job is a service, not a download.

Lemon Squeezy's [MoR docs](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/payments/merchant-of-record) say it acts as merchant of record and takes on tax, refunds, chargebacks and PCI scope for sales through the platform.

### Polar

Polar's [acceptable use policy](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/acceptable-use) (effective date on that page: October 1, 2026) is explicit about digital files. Acceptable products include:

- Software and SaaS.
- Digital products: templates, eBooks, PDFs, code, icons, fonts, design assets, photos, videos, audio and similar digital assets.
- Premium content and access: Discord server access, GitHub repository access, courses and content requiring a subscription.

Polar also lists eBooks under restricted businesses that require closer review. An ebook store is in scope, but it may get extra diligence rather than automatic approval.

Polar says it serves software companies and is not designed for primary human services or physical goods. Human services and physical products are on the prohibited list.

### Dodo Payments

Dodo's [merchant acceptance policy](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/merchant-acceptance) says it welcomes SaaS and AI products, digital goods, and templates, plugins and apps. That is a clear yes for templates and other finished digital goods.

E-books and written digital publications, including guides, playbooks, reports or downloadable written content, are listed among categories that often require extra review. Courses are not named as a welcomed or prohibited category on that page.

Dodo prohibits manual digital services where most of the value is human labour. The examples given are custom design, development, coaching, freelancing or consulting. Physical goods are prohibited, including merchandise bundled with a digital product. Dodo also says it may decline digital products with limited or unclear value, and gives AI-generated PDFs with little value and thin templates marked up heavily as examples.

A separate "might support" category covers productised services: a fixed, pre-defined digital product delivered the same way for every buyer, which may include limited, repeatable human operational work as long as it is not customized or consultative. That still goes through extra review. Live, custom coaching is on the prohibited side of the line.

### Stripe Managed Payments

Stripe's [Managed Payments eligibility docs](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/managed-payments/eligibility) support digital products. Supported categories include software, digital media such as audiobooks, e-books, digital magazines and newspapers, audio and video content, and digital images or artwork, plus online courses and training.

Products must be fully automated digital goods. Stripe says that if the service involves human intervention, such as live 1-1 coaching, it does not qualify. Physical goods, professional services (consulting, marketing, design, development or tech support) and live in-person events are unsupported.

The business must be based in a supported location (US, Canada, a listed set of European countries, Australia, Hong Kong, Japan or Singapore). Connect platforms and Express accounts are not supported. Stripe reviews each business for access. The Managed Payments fee is charged in addition to Payments fees; the public percentage was not clearly shown on the [pricing page](https://stripe.com/pricing) content we could read on 2026-11-07.

## How to choose for ebooks, templates and courses

| Situation | Start with (still verify) |
| --- | --- |
| Ebook or PDF download | Lemon Squeezy (named on the acceptable list), Polar (named, with extra review for eBooks) or Dodo Payments (extra review for e-books) |
| Templates or design assets | Lemon Squeezy (design assets), Polar (templates and design assets named) or Dodo Payments (templates named) |
| Pre-recorded course or course membership | Polar (courses named), Lemon Squeezy (courses mentioned on the pricing FAQ and activate-your-store docs) or Paddle (courses mentioned on digital-product pages). Stripe Managed Payments lists online courses and training. Dodo does not name courses. |
| Many non-US buyers and you want one flat published rate | Paddle, after you confirm your catalog is a fit |
| US-card-heavy one-time downloads and you want the lowest published US card rate among these four | Dodo Payments (no subscription add-on on a one-time sale) |
| Already on Polar before May 27, 2026 and still on Early Member | Polar Early Member rate, until you leave it |
| Business based in a Managed Payments location and you already use Stripe | Stripe Managed Payments, after eligibility review |
| Live coaching, custom design, consulting or other human-delivered services as the main product | None of these MoRs are a fit per their own policies |
| Physical books, merch or other goods that need shipping | None of these MoRs are a fit per their own policies |

Country eligibility is a separate gate from product fit. Seller-country detail for these providers is covered in [Paddle alternatives](/guides/paddle-alternatives/), [Lemon Squeezy supported countries](/guides/lemon-squeezy-supported-countries/) and [Dodo Payments supported countries](/guides/dodo-payments-supported-countries/).

## What this page does not cover

- Your home-country income tax on MoR payouts. Ask a local tax professional.
- Exact payout timing and payout fees beyond what each fee page states. See the comparison guides linked above for payout tables checked on earlier dates.
- Approval odds. Meeting the published product and country rules does not guarantee an account.
- Platforms that are not in this directory's provider set. This page sticks to Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments and Stripe Managed Payments.

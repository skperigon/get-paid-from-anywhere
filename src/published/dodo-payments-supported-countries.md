Dodo Payments is a merchant of record: it sells to your customers, handles sales tax and VAT, and pays you out. Before you build on it, check one thing first: whether you are eligible. Dodo's rules are stricter than a simple country list, and they changed in March 2026.

## How Dodo decides who can sell

According to Dodo's [accepted countries page](https://docs.dodopayments.com/miscellaneous/accepted-countries-and-territories):

- **Individuals** are checked on the country of their government ID.
- **Companies** are checked on the country of incorporation **and** the government ID country of every director and beneficial owner.
- Registering a company in a supported country does not, by itself, make you eligible.

That last point matters if you were planning to form a US LLC to get around a country restriction. With Dodo, the founders' ID countries still count.

## Grandfathered countries

Dodo applies a grandfathered status to some countries, with a policy dated March 23, 2026. Existing merchants from these countries are kept. New merchants from them are not eligible:

Bangladesh, Egypt, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Morocco, Nauru, Nigeria, Tuvalu and Ukraine.

## What Dodo charges

From Dodo's [pricing page](https://dodopayments.com/pricing):

- 4% + 40 cents for US domestic cards
- plus 1.5% for international cards and plus 0.5% for subscriptions
- 4% + 15 cents for India domestic INR payments
- plus 3% for PayPal and for buy now, pay later
- $30 per dispute, $1 per refund, $25 for USD SWIFT payouts

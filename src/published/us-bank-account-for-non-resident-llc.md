A US LLC does not automatically come with a US bank account. Mercury, Wise Business, Airwallex and Relay each publish their own rules for non-resident owners. Facts below come from those pages, checked on 2026-11-05. Eligibility changes, and applying does not guarantee approval, so verify with the provider before you rely on any option.

This page is about business accounts for a US-registered company. It is not tax, legal or immigration advice.

## What usually decides eligibility

Across these providers, the same three questions come up:

1. Is the company formed and registered in the US (or a US territory)?
2. Where do the founders and beneficial owners live (and, for some providers, hold citizenship)?
3. What address counts as the company's real place of business?

Registered agent addresses, P.O. boxes and virtual mailboxes are commonly rejected as operating addresses. Several providers also publish industry bans (crypto exchanges, cannabis, gambling and similar). Those lists are on each provider's own site.

## Mercury

Mercury's [eligibility page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility-and-requirements-for-opening-a-Mercury-account) says:

- The company must be formed and registered in the United States or a US territory.
- Founders do not need to be US citizens or residents.
- The business must have existing or planned operations in the US.
- The principal place of business can be a US or international address. Residential addresses are accepted. Registered agent addresses, P.O. boxes and UPS Store addresses are not.
- Mercury is a fintech company, not an FDIC-insured bank. Banking services are provided through Choice Financial Group and Column N.A., Members FDIC.

Mercury's [prohibited countries page](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries) says it cannot open accounts for founders who live in, or businesses that operate or do business in, the listed countries and regions. The rule is based on residence, not citizenship. Citizens of a listed country who currently reside elsewhere may still be eligible after review.

Sanctioned entries on that page (checked 2026-11-05): Cuba, Iran, North Korea, and Russian-occupied regions of Ukraine (Crimea, Donetsk, Kherson, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia).

Prohibited countries on that page (checked 2026-11-05): Afghanistan, Albania, Angola, Bangladesh, Belarus, Bhutan, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cambodia, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Congo (the Democratic Republic of the), Congo (the), Croatia, Eritrea, Gambia, Haiti, Indonesia, Iraq, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lebanon, Lesotho, Liberia, Libya, Maldives, Mali, Mozambique, Myanmar (Burma), Nepal, Nicaragua, Nigeria, Pakistan, Palestine (State of), Philippines, Russia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Vanuatu, Venezuela, Vietnam, Yemen, Zimbabwe.

## Wise Business

Wise is not a bank. Its help centre describes Wise Business as an account for receiving and sending money, with local account details in several currencies.

From [Can my business use Wise?](https://wise.com/help/articles/2977974/can-my-business-use-wise):

- Wise works for sole traders, freelancers, limited and public companies, and some partnerships.
- When you add your details, Wise says it will tell you whether it supports businesses in your country.
- Wise does not work for businesses in cryptocurrency, tobacco, adult content and other prohibited activities, or for businesses with bearer shares.

From [Where can I use Wise?](https://wise.com/help/articles/2978049/where-can-i-use-wise), places Wise does not support include Afghanistan, Belarus, Burundi, Central African Republic, Chad, Congo, Democratic Republic of the Congo, Cuba, Eritrea, Iran, Iraq, North Korea, Libya, Myanmar, Somalia, Republic of South Sudan, Russia, Sudan, Syria, Yemen, Venezuela, and the Ukraine regions Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia.

### USD account details

[Can I get USD account details?](https://wise.com/help/articles/2810318/can-i-get-usd-account-details) says you can get USD details for a personal or Wise Business account if your address is in a country where you can open that currency. The same page lists address locations where USD details are unavailable, including Bangladesh, Nigeria, Pakistan, the United Arab Emirates, Türkiye, Nevada, and others.

For Wise Business specifically, that page also says you cannot get USD account details if:

- your US business has an address in Bangladesh, Cameroon, China, Nigeria or Pakistan, or
- your personal profile has an address in Bangladesh, Cameroon, China, Nigeria, Pakistan or Ukraine.

[How do I receive money with my USD account details?](https://wise.com/help/articles/2827506/how-do-i-receive-money-with-my-usd-account-details) says USD details give you an account and routing number to receive USD. Domestic payments can arrive by ACH or wire from the US. International payments can arrive by Swift in USD. Wise says this is not a bank account. Receiving fees on that page: no fee for ACH, and 6.11 USD for domestic wires and for Swift.

Wise's [fees for Wise Business](https://wise.com/help/articles/2977969/fees-for-using-wise-business) page says sign-up is free for regions other than Brazil (registration fee) and India (no extra charge to get account details), and that other regions pay a fee to get account details that depends on where the business is registered. Wise's [US requirements blog](https://wise.com/us/blog/wise-business-account-requirements-us) (last updated 31 July 2026) states a one-time fee of 31 USD for account details for US businesses, and says Wise cannot onboard businesses located in Nevada or in US territories (American Samoa, Guam, Mariana Islands, US Virgin Islands). Check Wise's pricing page for the amount that applies to you.

## Airwallex

Airwallex onboards by where the business is registered. Its [eligible countries](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4408591334937-Eligible-Countries-and-Territories) table lists the United States of America with Airwallex Entity `UNITED STATES OF AMERICA`. Registration in a listed country does not guarantee approval.

For US companies, Airwallex's [US documents guide](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4419267227417-Documents-required-for-companies-in-the-US) FAQ says:

- You can apply if the business is registered in the US, even if you or your owners live outside the US. Airwallex looks at where each owner lives, not nationality.
- The business must be registered in the US and authorized to do business there.
- The business needs a genuine principal place of business in the US. E-commerce businesses and businesses incorporated within the last year have an exception that can allow a registered agent's address as that principal place of business. PO boxes, virtual mailboxes and mailbox rental services are still not accepted.
- No owner or Ultimate Beneficial Owner may live in a restricted country or territory (see Airwallex's eligible countries article).
- For a company, partnership or other entity type, an SSN or ITIN is not required. Non-US persons verify with a passport or other government-issued photo ID, and enter the business EIN as its Tax ID.
- Applying does not guarantee approval, and Airwallex says it cannot confirm eligibility before you apply.

Airwallex's [eligibility overview](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/900001757026-Eligibility-to-open-an-account) adds that the business must be active and in good standing, must pass industry and sanctions checks, and may still be declined after risk review.

## Relay

Relay's [FAQ](https://relayfi.com/faqs/) says it supports US corporations, LLCs, general partnerships and sole proprietors, and business owners who are citizens and residents of over 200 countries, as long as the business has an operating presence in the US. Owners from countries on its prohibited list are excluded.

[Prohibited Countries](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/10239600121748-Prohibited-Countries/) (updated 11 August 2026) says Relay cannot open an account for any business owner who holds citizenship or residency in: Afghanistan, Belarus, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Burkina Faso, Burma (Myanmar), Burundi, Cambodia, Central African Republic, Congo (Democratic Republic), Cuba, Ethiopia, Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, Iran, Iraq, Lebanon, Libya, Mali, Nicaragua, Nigeria, North Korea, Pakistan, Russia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, Venezuela, Yemen, Zimbabwe. The restriction applies to owners named on the application, including beneficial owners, regardless of where the business is registered. The same page says US entities with non-US citizen owners are permitted in most cases, subject to that list.

[Required documents by entity type](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/38049251551636-Required-Documents-and-Organization-Details-to-Open-a-Relay-Account-by-Entity-Type/) says Relay accepts US-registered businesses owned by non-US citizens or residents when the business has an operating presence in the US. For every beneficial owner it lists a government-issued photo ID, SSN or passport number, and a physical US address (no PO boxes or virtual mailboxes). Non-US resident beneficial owners also provide source of income, whether the business is new or established, annual personal income, and countries of operation.

Relay's own [How to open a Relay account](https://support.relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/38050807932436-How-to-Open-a-Relay-Business-Account) page says every beneficial owner must have a valid SSN. That conflicts with the required-documents page, which allows a passport number. Treat SSN requirements for non-US owners as unknown until Relay confirms your case.

Relay says deposits are available through Thread Bank, Member FDIC.

## How to choose

| Situation | Providers to check first (still verify) |
| --- | --- |
| You live outside Mercury's prohibited list and have a US LLC with a real operating address | Mercury |
| You mainly need USD receiving details and multi-currency transfers, not a full US bank feature set | Wise Business, if your address allows USD details |
| Your company is US-registered, you can show a US principal place of business (or fit an Airwallex exception), and no UBO lives in a restricted country | Airwallex |
| Your company is US-registered with US operating presence, owners are outside Relay's prohibited list, and you can meet Relay's address and ID rules | Relay |

If your country of residence is blocked at one provider, another provider's list may still allow you. Do not assume a formation service's marketing claims about "banking partners" without reading the bank or fintech's own eligibility pages.

## Before you apply

- Confirm your residence and citizenship against each provider's current country page.
- Use a principal or trading address the provider accepts. Registered agent and virtual mailbox addresses fail at several of these firms.
- Have your EIN, formation documents, and ID for every 25%+ owner ready.
- Expect manual review. Meeting the published rules does not guarantee approval.
- Country lists, fees and partner banks change. Re-check the linked pages on the day you apply.

Formation services tracked elsewhere on this site include [doola](/provider/doola/), [Firstbase](/provider/firstbase/) and [Northwest Registered Agent](/provider/northwest-registered-agent/). This page does not treat their marketing claims about bank partners as eligibility proof.
